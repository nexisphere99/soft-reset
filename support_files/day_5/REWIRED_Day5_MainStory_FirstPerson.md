## PASSAGE: D05_Morning

### Scene: Preparing for Work

The alarm goes off at seven and I lie there staring at the ceiling fist. Five days since that stain meant anything normal. Five days since Marcus paid rent and swallowed a pill and woke up someone else entirely.

Today I go back to DashDrop. Today Owen sees me.

I roll out of bed and my feet hit cold floor and my hair falls across my face and both of those things feel unremarkable now. That's the strangest part. Not the breasts or the hips or the voice. The strangest part is how quickly strange becomes Tuesday.

Shower first. Hot water finally, because Mrs. Park got the plumber out on Day 3 and charged me forty bucks for it. I stand under the stream and wash my hair with the coconut shampoo from the corner store and the lather smells like a vacation I can't afford. Conditioner after. Leave it in two minutes while I shave my legs, which takes longer than it should because my hands are shaking and it's not from the razor.

I'm nervous. Genuinely, stomach-flipping, might-throw-up nervous. The kind of nervous I used to get before exams back when I still went to class. Except this exam is walking into a dispatch office full of guys who knew me as Marcus and hoping the floor doesn't open up.

Towel off. Lotion. I smooth it across my calves and my skin drinks the moisture in and for a second I just stand there with one foot up on the toilet lid, looking at my own leg like it belongs to somebody I'm getting to know. Soft. Smooth. Mine, allegedly.

Clothes. I've thought about this. Overthought it, probably. Sports bra first. The one from Marlene's shop, the plain black one that holds everything flat enough to not bounce when I lean over a scooter. Black t-shirt, fitted but not tight. Jeans. The jeans fit different now, snug at the hip and the thigh and loose at the ankle. Five days ago these were Marcus's jeans. Now they're jeans that happen to be on a woman.

The DashDrop jacket comes off the hook. Orange polyester, reflective strip on the back, smells like exhaust and old coffee. I pull it on and the shoulders swim. Too wide. The waist pulls at my hips. I zip it anyway. It'll have to do until I can swap it.

Hair. This is new territory. I pull it back into a low ponytail and use one of the black elastics I bought at the corner store on a whim two days ago. First ponytail. The pull at my temples feels intentional in a way that nothing about my hair ever did when it was short. Practical, not cute. But it shows my face clearly. My female face. Cheekbones higher than Marcus ever had. Jaw softer. Lips fuller. Eyes the same hazel-green but somehow wider now, like they opened up when the rest of my face changed.

I look at the mirror. A girl in a delivery jacket looks back.

She looks scared.

Helmet. Keys. Phone. Deep breath that fills lungs that sit higher in a chest that curves where it used to be flat.

I go.

## PASSAGE: D05_DashDrop_Arrival

### Scene: DashDrop HQ — Facing Owen

The dispatch office sits behind a strip mall on Garfield, wedged between a vape shop and a laundromat that's been "temporarily closed" since before I started working here. The parking lot smells like burned coffee and two-stroke exhaust from the scooter fleet. Three other drivers lounge against the wall outside, scrolling phones, none of them people I know well enough to worry about.

I park. Kill the motor. Sit on the scooter for a full minute, gripping the handlebars, watching my knuckles go white. In my pocket, the new ID. The one with the F on it and the photo that looks like me now instead of the person I used to be. NovaCure did the paperwork fast. Suspiciously fast. Like they'd done it before.

My legs carry me inside before my brain agrees to it.

The office is exactly the same. Corkboard with shift schedules pinned crookedly. A desk buried in takeout menus and dispatch tablets. A calendar on the wall still turned to two months ago. The industrial coffee pot that produces something technically drinkable but spiritually hostile.

Owen stands behind the counter sorting route sheets. Owen is thirty-four, ex-Army, has a mustache he thinks makes him look like a 70s cop but actually makes him look like a guy who lost a bet. He's been my manager for eight months. He calls everyone "buddy" regardless of gender, which is either progressive or lazy depending on how charitable you feel.

He looks up when the door chimes.

Full stop. Dead halt. His brain visibly crashes and reboots behind his eyes. He looks at me. At the ID badge on the counter. Back at me. Down at the dispatch tablet, like maybe the answer is there. Back at me.

I slide the new legal ID across the counter. My hand is steady. That surprises me.

"I need to update my profile."

Owen picks up the ID. Looks at the photo. Looks at me. The photo is maybe two days old, taken in one of those pharmacy photo booths where the fluorescent light makes everyone look like a suspect. But it's clearly me. Clearly female. Clearly not Marcus.

"So... this is... you're..."

"Yeah."

Processing. I can almost hear the fans spinning behind his forehead. Owen is not a complicated man. Owen likes football, his daughter's soccer games, and cheap beer. Owen's worldview does not include a framework for his twenty-year-old delivery driver showing up as a woman on a Wednesday morning.

But Owen is also, and I will remember this for the rest of my life, a fundamentally decent human being.

"...Okay." He sets the ID down. "You still know the routes?"

"Yeah."

"Cool. I'll update the system." He turns toward the back room, stops, turns back. "You need a smaller jacket? That one's swimming on you."

Something hot and wet presses behind my eyes and I blink it back so fast it never reaches my cheeks.

"A smaller jacket would be great."

He disappears into the storeroom. I hear boxes shifting, hangers sliding. He comes back with a women's small DashDrop jacket. Same ugly orange. Same reflective strip. But when I put it on, it fits. The shoulders sit where shoulders should sit. The waist follows a curve instead of hanging straight down. The zipper doesn't gap at my chest.

"Thanks, Owen."

He waves me off. "Don't mention it. Literally. I got no idea how to talk about this so I'm just gonna pretend it's normal. That cool?"

"That's perfect."

He nods once. Goes back to sorting route sheets. And that's it. No speech. No questions. No awkward attempt at wokeness. Just a guy who decided this was above his pay grade and chose the path of least drama, which accidentally made it the kindest thing anyone's done for me in five days.

He never brings it up again. Treats me exactly the same as before. Still calls me "buddy," except now in a slightly higher pitch, like he's not sure if the word still applies.

It does. He's the best.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery1

### Scene: First Delivery Shift as Female — Delivery 1

**Sushi place to an office building. Routine.**

The route is the same. Highway to Oak, left on Third, pull around to the service entrance of the Lexington office park. I've done this run six times as Marcus. My body knows the turns.

What my body doesn't know is how the wind hits differently when your hair is in a ponytail instead of buzzed short. The way it whips the tail against the back of my neck. The way the helmet sits different with more hair underneath, pressing at the temples. Small things. Stupid things. Things that shouldn't matter but do because they keep reminding me that the person on this scooter is not the person who was on this scooter last week.

I park at the loading dock, grab the bag, take the service elevator to the seventh floor. The receptionist is a woman in her fifties with reading glasses on a chain. She signs the receipt without looking up from her monitor.

"Thanks, hon."

"You're welcome."

She doesn't look twice. Doesn't register anything unusual. I'm just a delivery girl dropping off California rolls on a Wednesday afternoon. Background character in someone else's day.

I walk back to the elevator and my reflection passes in the hallway glass and for one disorienting second I almost nod at the woman walking toward me before I realize she's me.

Tip: $3. Standard. Same as Marcus would've gotten.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery2

### Scene: Delivery 2 — Burger joint to the Hot Couple's apartment (Trent callback)

The address pops up on my dispatch screen and my stomach does something complicated. I know this building. I know this apartment. Specifically, I know the couple inside it, because five days ago I delivered their pizza while they were clearly in the middle of having sex. She answered the door in an oversized shirt and nothing else. He was shirtless behind her, grinning. They tipped me ten bucks and I walked away feeling lonely in a way that sat in my chest for hours.

That was Marcus. This is me.

I take the elevator up. Knock. Wait.

The door opens. It's him. Alone this time. His girlfriend must be out. He's wearing grey sweats and a tank top that shows off arms he clearly works on. He was expecting a different delivery person. Was probably expecting Marcus, if he remembered Marcus at all.

He sees me. Does a double-take so quick it almost looks like a glitch. His eyes track from my face to the DashDrop bag to my body and back to my face in a route that takes about half a second and covers a lot of territory.

"Hey, you're new? Haven't seen you before."

The lie comes easy. "Yeah, just started."

He leans against the doorframe. Tips his chin up. Smiles. It's the kind of smile that knows exactly what it's doing and has been doing it successfully since high school. "I'm Trent."

"Mia."

"Mia." He says it back like he's tasting it. "Cool. You should come by sometime. We have, like, parties and stuff."

He hands me the tip. Fifteen dollars. Five more than he gave Marcus. The math on that is not subtle.

"Thanks, Trent."

I walk away. I can feel his eyes on my ass. Not a suspicion. A certainty. A physical weight tracing the shape of the jeans across my hips and down. Five days ago he didn't look twice at the dude in the same orange jacket. Today he's watching me walk to the elevator with the kind of attention that starts in the eyes and ends somewhere lower.

I press the elevator button and wait and don't turn around and my face is hot and I don't know if it's anger or embarrassment or something else entirely. Something that might be the beginning of liking being looked at.

The elevator doors close. I breathe.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery3

### Scene: Delivery 3 — Smoothie shop to Sunrise Yoga Studio

The yoga studio is in a converted storefront on Bell Street, sandwiched between a dry cleaner and a place that sells crystals. The windows are covered in mandala decals and through the glass I can see hardwood floors and a wall of mirrors and a woman mid-warrior-pose who looks like she could bend in ways that would send me to the emergency room.

I carry the smoothie order inside. The smell hits me first. Incense, clean sweat, eucalyptus. The sound second. Some kind of ambient music that's either deeply relaxing or deeply annoying depending on your tolerance for synthesized wind chimes.

"Oh yay, my mango bliss!"

She bounces over from behind a small reception desk. Sage. That's the name on the order. She's about thirty, athletic, deeply tan, with blonde hair in two long braids and the kind of energy that makes you tired just standing near her. She radiates health the way a space heater radiates warmth, aggressively and from every direction.

"I'm Sage." She takes the smoothie bag and pulls it open like it's a present. "Oh my god, you're a lifesaver. I've been teaching since seven and I forgot to eat and I was about to start gnawing on a yoga block."

She looks at my jacket. Then at me. Tilts her head the way a golden retriever does when you make a weird noise.

"You look like you need a stretch."

"I'm okay."

"No, I mean like, your shoulders are up by your ears. That's cortisol. First class is free if you ever want to try." She grabs a flyer from the desk and hands it to me. It's printed on lavender paper and says SUNRISE YOGA in a font that's trying very hard to look hand-lettered. Class schedule on the back. "I teach the morning flow and the sunset yin. The morning one will change your life. The sunset one will put you to sleep. Both good things."

I take the flyer because refusing would require more energy than accepting. "Thanks."

"I'm serious. Come in. You've got good bones for it." She sips her smoothie through a metal straw and makes a sound that's either satisfaction or something more private. "Mmmm. Okay. Perfection. Thank you..."

"Mia."

"Mia! Love it. Come to class, Mia. Your spine will thank me."

I leave with the flyer folded in my back pocket. Behind me, Sage has already turned back to her studio, smoothie in hand, humming something that sounds like it was composed by dolphins.

Tip: $8. Generous for a smoothie order. She probably tips everyone well. She seems like the type.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery4

### Scene: Delivery 4 — Thai food to a college dorm

The college dorms are brutalist concrete towers that look like someone designed a parking garage and then said "what if people lived here." I used to walk past them when I was still enrolled. Used to think they looked depressing from outside. Now I think they look depressing from inside too, because I'm standing in a hallway that smells like Febreze and desperation, knocking on door 412.

Music thumps behind the door. Bass. Something with a lot of 808.

The door swings open and a cloud of something sweet and illegal rolls out. Behind the cloud stands a guy in basketball shorts and no shirt. He's maybe twenty, built like he just discovered the gym six months ago and hasn't stopped going since. His eyes are red. His grin is wide. He is profoundly, emphatically baked.

"YOOOOO! Food's here!" He turns back to the room. "Kevin, food's here, bro!"

Another voice from inside. "I'm Kevin, dumbass. You're the one who ordered it."

The guy at the door blinks. Processes. "Oh shit. Right. I'm Kevin." He looks at me. "Sorry, I'm Kevin. I ordered this."

"Pad Thai and spring rolls."

"That's the one!" He takes the bag like I'm handing him an award. "You're the best. Hey, what's your name?"

"Mia."

"Mia! Sick name. Hey Mia, you want to come in? We're playing Mario Kart and Deshawn brought edibles. They're like, really good edibles. His cousin makes them."

From inside the room, a third voice. "Bro, don't invite the delivery girl in. That's weird."

Kevin turns back. "Why is that weird? She seems cool!"

"Kevin. No."

Kevin turns back to me. "Sorry. My roommates are socially rigid." He says it like he learned the phrase yesterday and is very proud of it. "But hey, if you ever want to, like, hang out or whatever. Room 412. We're here, like, always."

He tips me twelve dollars on a sixteen-dollar order, which is either very generous or a math error. Based on Kevin, probably both.

I leave. Behind me, I hear Kevin announce to the room, "She was cute, right? Am I wrong? I'm not wrong."

I'm smiling in the elevator. I didn't expect that.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery5

### Scene: Delivery 5 — Sandwich shop to a residential address

The house is small. Single story. A lawn that needs mowing and a porch with a plastic tricycle tipped on its side. The garden hose is coiled beside the front steps like a green snake. There are crayon drawings taped to the inside of the front window.

I knock. The door opens and a woman stands there with a toddler on her hip. She's maybe twenty-five, dark circles under her eyes, hair in a messy bun that's less "intentional messy" and more "I haven't slept in three days messy." The kid is chewing on a plastic dinosaur and staring at me with the profound suspicion that toddlers reserve for all strangers.

"Oh thank god." She shifts the kid to her other hip and reaches for the bag. "I have been thinking about this sandwich since nine this morning. I placed the order at eleven and then stared at the tracking screen for forty-five minutes like a psychopath."

"Two clubs and a cookie?"

"The cookie is mine. The clubs are for me and my future self at midnight when I'm up with this one." She jerks her head at the toddler, who responds by throwing the dinosaur on the floor. She doesn't pick it up. Practiced indifference. "Sorry about him. He throws things. It's his whole personality right now."

"It's fine."

She looks at me. Really looks. Not the way Trent looked, calculating and weighted. This is the look of one tired person recognizing something in another tired person.

"Long day?"

"Getting there."

"Tell me about it." She fishes a ten out of her pocket. "I'm Jess, by the way. If you ever deliver here again, and you probably will because I am a creature of habit and this sandwich shop is the only thing keeping me sane, just know the door might take a minute because small humans are slow."

"I'm Mia."

"Mia." She smiles. It's a good smile. Exhausted but genuine. "Nice to meet you, Mia. Thanks for the food."

The door closes. Through the window I can see her walking back toward a kitchen table covered in crayons and sippy cups, the sandwich bag held up like a trophy. The kid toddles after her, dinosaur forgotten on the porch.

I pick up the dinosaur. Set it on the railing where she'll see it. Get back on my scooter.

Tip: $10. She can probably barely afford it. I appreciate it more for that.

## PASSAGE: D05_Delivery_End

### Scene: Shift Wrap-Up

Five deliveries. Eighty-seven dollars in tips. Best day I've ever had at DashDrop.

I park the scooter at the dispatch lot and sit there doing the math I don't want to do. Marcus averaged fifty to sixty on a good shift. Same routes. Same distances. Same mediocre food handed to the same hungry people. The only variable that changed is the body doing the handing.

Eighty-seven. Twenty-seven more than usual. Because I'm pretty now. Because Trent tipped extra to keep the cute delivery girl coming back. Because Kevin was stoned and friendly and my face made him friendlier. Because being a woman in a service job comes with a tax levied on the men you serve, paid in crumpled bills and lingering eye contact.

This is a fact of the world I never had to confront before. Attractiveness has a market value. Marcus knew this intellectually. Mia is learning it in her tip jar.

I don't know how to feel about it. So I don't. I pocket the money and head downtown.

## PASSAGE: D05_Downtown_Explore

### Scene: Downtown Exploration — Discovering New Locations

The route from DashDrop to my apartment goes through downtown, and normally I take it straight, no detours, because normally I want to get home and eat and stare at the wall. But today I'm wired. The nervous energy from this morning burned off somewhere around Delivery 3 and what's left is something lighter. Not confidence exactly. More like proof of concept. I went outside. People saw me. Nobody screamed. The world continued.

So instead of turning left on Barker toward home, I go straight. Past the library. Past the courthouse. Into the stretch of downtown where the chain stores gave up and the independent shops took over, the ones with hand-painted signs and irregular hours and owners who smoke out back.

I'm riding slow. Looking. Seeing the city the way you see a place you think you might actually live in, rather than just survive in.

## PASSAGE: D05_BlackLine_Discover

### Scene: Black Line Studio

I notice it because of the light. Late afternoon sun hits the storefront at an angle that turns the plate glass into a screen, and through it I can see the interior lit warm and golden. Clean. Not the grungy tattoo parlor I would have expected. This place looks like a gallery that happens to do tattoos.

BLACK LINE STUDIO. The sign is simple. Black letters on white. No skulls, no flames, no tribal bullshit. Just a name and a window full of framed flash art that looks more like illustration than tattoo design.

Through the glass I can see someone working. A man. Lean, tattooed, focused on a client's arm with the kind of concentration that bends the air around him. I can't see his face clearly but I can see his hands, and they move with a precision that makes the needle look like a pen.

I slow down. Something about this place. I don't know what. I don't have tattoos. Marcus never wanted one. But Mia stops her scooter and looks through the window for a full ten seconds longer than someone who doesn't want a tattoo should.

I pull away. File it somewhere in the back of my head under "later."

The location sits in my mental map now. Black Line Studio. Downtown, between a bookshop and a place that sells vinyl records. Not enterable. Not yet. But noticed.

## PASSAGE: D05_Grounded_Discover

### Scene: Grounded Coffee House — Meeting River Chen

Two more blocks and the smell gets me before the sign does. Coffee. Real coffee, not the chemical nonsense from DashDrop's industrial pot. The deep, roasted, slightly chocolaty smell of beans that someone actually cared about grinding.

GROUNDED COFFEE HOUSE. Open. Busy. Warm light through big windows. People at laptops, people reading, people talking over cups that trail steam. The kind of place that exists in every college town but this one looks good. Lived in. Like the furniture was chosen for comfort instead of durability.

I need caffeine. That's my excuse and it's valid. I park the scooter, pull off my helmet, shake out my ponytail, and walk in.

The smell is better inside. Coffee and something baking. Cinnamon, maybe. Brown sugar. The floor is reclaimed wood and the walls are exposed brick and there's a chalkboard menu behind the counter that someone drew little leaf designs on. A string of Edison bulbs runs across the ceiling. It should feel tryhard. It doesn't. It feels like someone's very specific, very warm living room that happens to serve lattes.

Behind the counter stands a guy. Maybe twenty-two. Shaggy black hair pushed under a grey beanie. He's got a pronoun pin on his apron, small, he/him, the kind you have to be looking for to notice. Easy smile. Quick hands. He's steaming milk and chatting with the woman at the register at the same time, making both look effortless.

I join the short line. When I reach the front, he turns the full wattage of that smile on me.

"Hey! What can I get you?"

His name tag says RIVER. The handwriting is his own, slightly crooked, like he wrote it on a moving bus.

"Latte, please. Oat milk if you have it."

"We do. Good choice." He grabs a cup, writes something on it. "Size?"

"Medium. Regular."

"Coming right up."

He makes it fast and well. The pour is practiced. When he sets it on the counter, there's a little swirl in the foam. Not a full latte art heart or leaf, just a swirl that says "I gave a shit about this specific cup." I appreciate that.

"I'm River, by the way." He leans on the counter for a half-second. Not lingering. Just present. "You look like you had a day."

"You have no idea."

"Those are the ones that make the coffee taste best." The smile again. Then a new customer appears behind me and he's already moving, already shifting attention, but he glances back. "Enjoy."

I take the latte to a corner booth. The seat is old leather, cracked and soft. I sit down and wrap my hands around the cup and drink.

It's perfect. Not in a transcendent way. In a "this is exactly what coffee should taste like and I haven't had that in a long time" way. Hot without burning. Sweet without sugar. The oat milk gives it this velvety thing that regular milk never does.

I sit there for twenty minutes. Watching people. A woman typing furiously on a laptop. Two students sharing a pastry and arguing about something that involves a lot of hand gestures. An older man reading a paperback with a cracked spine. River behind the counter, laughing at something a coworker said.

For the first time in five days, peace. Not happiness. Not the absence of fear. Just a moment where the noise in my head drops to a hum instead of a roar and I can sit in a public place and drink coffee and be a person who drinks coffee in public places.

The latte costs five dollars. I leave a two-dollar tip. I'll be back.

## PASSAGE: D05_Evening_Home

### Scene: Home — Processing the Day

I get home at six. Lock the door. Drop my bag. Stand in the kitchen doing nothing for about ninety seconds, which is how long it takes for the adrenaline of an entire day of public-facing womanhood to finish leaving my body.

Then I cook. Eggs on toast. Basic but protein. I eat standing at the counter because sitting down feels too official for a meal this simple. The eggs taste good. The toast is burnt on one edge. I eat the burnt edge too because wasting food when you have $1,545 in the bank and a landlord who will evict you for fun feels like tempting fate.

The math from today keeps circling. Eighty-seven dollars in tips. Twenty-seven more than Marcus averaged. Because I'm attractive now. Because the world pays pretty women a little more and uglier women a little less and men whatever they want to pay men, and none of this is fair and all of it is true.

I should feel something about this. Outrage, maybe. Or gratitude. Or guilt. Instead I feel practical. I feel like a person who just learned the exchange rate in a country they didn't choose to move to.

## PASSAGE: D05_Nolan_Call

### Scene: Nolan Won't Wait

The phone rings at seven-thirty. Nolan's name on the screen. His contact photo is from freshman year, both of us in those stupid orientation shirts, both of us grinning like we'd just been let off leash.

I stare at it. Three rings. Four. I can't dodge him forever. He's been texting every day since the transformation and my responses have gotten shorter and vaguer and eventually he's going to show up at my door whether I answer the phone or not. That's who Nolan is. He doesn't take hints. He takes doors off hinges.

I pick up.

"FINALLY." His voice blasts through the speaker loud enough to make me wince. "What the hell, dude? You've been MIA for like five days."

"I know. I... something happened." My voice. My new voice. Alto where it used to be baritone. Softer. I can hear him hearing it. A half-second pause where his brain stumbles.

"You sound weird. Are you sick?"

"Not exactly."

"Not exactly? What does 'not exactly' mean? Are you dying? Do you have cancer? Because if you have cancer I swear to god I will personally fight every cancer cell in your body. I'll throw hands with your tumors."

Despite everything, I almost smile. That's Nolan. Threatens to fistfight disease.

"I'm not dying. It's just... you need to see it. In person. Can you come over tomorrow?"

"See what? See it? What am I seeing? Are you being vague on purpose because I gotta tell you, it's working, I'm freaking out over here."

"I know. I'm sorry. Just come over. After your shift. Like six."

Silence. Long enough that I check the screen to make sure the call didn't drop.

"...Okay." His voice is different now. Quieter. Nolan is never quiet. When Nolan is quiet it means he's actually worried, not performatively worried. "Tomorrow at six. But Marcus, whatever this is, it's gonna be fine. You know that, right?"

Marcus. The name lands in my chest like a fist through a window. He still calls me Marcus. Of course he does. What else would he call me.

"Yeah. It's gonna be fine."

"Promise?"

"Promise."

He hangs up. I put the phone face-down on the counter and press both palms flat against the laminate and breathe.

Tomorrow, Nolan sees me. Nolan, who I've known since freshman orientation. Nolan, who sat next to me on the first day of Econ 101 and whispered "this professor sucks, wanna get food?" and became the closest thing I have to a best friend in this city. Nolan, who thinks he's coming to check on Marcus.

Tomorrow, he walks into this apartment and sees the girl his friend became.

I wash the dishes. I dry them. I put them away in the cabinet where they go and the whole time my hands are steady because if I let them shake now they might never stop.

## PASSAGE: D05_Night

### Scene: Before Sleep

I lie in bed. The ceiling fist stares down at me. I stare back.

Today I left the apartment and worked a shift and earned money and talked to people and nobody called the police. Nobody screamed. Nobody pointed. A receptionist called me "hon." A college kid called me "cute." A yoga instructor said I have good bones. A barista made me a latte and told me bad days make good coffee.

I am Mia. I went outside and the world accepted me as Mia. Not enthusiastically, not with fanfare, but with the casual indifference that is, it turns out, the highest form of acceptance. Nobody cared. Everyone moved on. I was just another girl on a scooter delivering sushi in an orange jacket.

Tomorrow, Nolan comes. Tomorrow is different. Tomorrow is someone who knew Marcus looking at Mia and trying to make those two people exist in the same body. That's harder than strangers. Strangers don't have context. Strangers don't have memories. Strangers don't look at you and see the ghost of who you used to be standing behind the person you are now.

But that's tomorrow.

Tonight, I pull the blanket up. I touch my ponytail, still in, still pulling gently at my temples. I should take it out. I leave it. It feels like something I earned.

My phone buzzes one more time. I don't check it. Whatever it is, it can wait until morning. My eyes close. Sleep comes faster than it has in days.

Eighty-seven dollars. Five deliveries. One kind manager. One stoned kid. One tired mom. One hot guy who stared at my ass. One yoga instructor who wants to fix my spine. One barista who makes good coffee.

Day five. Still here.
